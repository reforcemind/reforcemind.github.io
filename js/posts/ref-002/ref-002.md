## Structure-Adaptive Jacobian Recovery for Receding-Horizon MPC

In receding-horizon MPC, the inner optimisation at each planning step is a root-finding problem: find control $u \in \mathbb{R}^N$ such that $F(u) = q(u) - q^\star = 0$, where $q(u)$ denotes the state reached after applying $u$ through a differentiable simulator. Quasi-Newton methods maintain a running approximation $J_t \approx \partial F / \partial u \big|_{u_t}$ and update it via rank-1 secant corrections, avoiding the full Jacobian at every step.

The natural cost unit is the **Jacobian-Vector Product (JVP)**: one evaluation of $v \mapsto (\partial F / \partial u) v$ via forward-mode autodiff, at cost equivalent to one simulator rollout. A full finite-difference (FD) rebuild costs $N + 1$ JVPs. For a horizon of $T$ outer steps with $m$ discontinuity events, the cumulative FD penalty is $m(N+1)$ JVPs — linear in $N$ per event. The question is whether the *structure* of each discontinuity can reduce this.

First-order gradient baselines (Adam, SGD) require the same $N$ JVPs per step to construct a gradient, and in our evaluations converge 0 times out of 25 outer steps across all $N$, stalling at the discontinuity boundary. The solver is necessary; the question is how cheaply it can recover.

---

## 1. Discontinuity Archetypes

Before building recovery methods, we measured the singular structure of the Jacobian jump $E = J^\star_{\text{after}} - J_{t-1}$ at real events in MuJoCo/MJX.

**Gear events** (parametric, column-sparse): A gear-ratio perturbation on a subset $S \subseteq [N]$ of actuators produces a jump satisfying $E e_i = 0$ for all $i \notin S$. Measured singular spectrum is flat at rank $|S|$; unfuzzed column norms are identically zero to numerical precision. The discontinuity is column-localised.

**Contact events** (topological, low-rank dense): A tip-contact transition (ncon: $0 \to 1$) produces a jump with top-1 singular energy $\geq 0.98$, column norms uniformly nonzero (sparsity $= 0$). The jump is rank-$\approx 1$ and dense, $\approx 75\%$ symmetric.

These archetypes are structurally orthogonal. No single recovery mechanism is simultaneously optimal on both — the Hybrid Router (§3) resolves this by diagnosing the type first.

---

## 2. Tracking-Error Anomaly Detection

Scalar Lipschitz-ratio detectors — comparing $\|y_t\| / \|s_t\|$ across steps — are blind to jumps whose column support is orthogonal to the current search direction $s_t$. A rank-1 contact event, for instance, produces no signal if $E s_t \approx 0$.

The **tracking-error detector** evaluates, at each step, the Jacobian residual along a fresh probe direction $w$:

$$e_t(w) = \frac{\left\| \bigl(F(x + \delta w) - F(x)\bigr)/\delta \;-\; J_{t-1}\, w \right\|}{\| J_{t-1}\, w \|}$$

An anomaly is declared when $e_t(w) > \tau$. With $k_p$ probes per step drawn independently, the detection latency for a jump satisfying a separation $\|E e_i\| \geq \mu \|J_{t-1} e_i\|$ is $\Theta(N / k_p)$ steps in expectation. Increasing $k_p$ reduces latency at linear cost; the regret theorem (§4) makes this tradeoff explicit.

**Non-stationary variant (EWMA).** For slowly drifting dynamics, the threshold $\tau$ itself must track the baseline level. The EWMA detector maintains $\hat{L}_t = \lambda L_t + (1-\lambda)\hat{L}_{t-1}$ on the detrended residual $L_t = e_t / \hat{z}_{t-1}$, where $\hat{z}_{t-1}$ is the EWMA of the nominal level. For jumps $\Delta \geq 8\sigma$, detection is instantaneous regardless of variant; for small persistent drift ($\Delta \sim 2\sigma$) the EWMA reduces spurious rebuilds by 14–20% compared to windowed MAD. This is a bias–variance tradeoff, not a dominance: at $\Delta \sim 2\sigma$, the EWMA absorbs the anomaly into its level estimate (14% miss rate).

## 3. Recovery

### 3a. Group-Testing for Column-Sparse Jumps

Let $S \subseteq [N]$, $|S| = r$, denote the support of the column-sparse jump. A **block test** with mask $w$ supported on $G \subseteq [N]$ returns the action $E w$ in one JVP, from which one reads the binary signal $\mathbf{1}[\|E w\| / \|J_{t-1} w\| \geq \epsilon]$ for whether $G \cap S \neq \emptyset$. Binary-splitting group testing (Hwang's adaptive algorithm) identifies $S$ and recovers exact changed columns $\{E e_i\}_{i \in S}$ in $\mathcal{O}(r \log(N/r))$ probes. The rebuilt Jacobian is then exact:

$$J_{\text{new}} = J_{\text{stale}} + \sum_{i \in S} (E e_i)\, e_i^\top, \qquad J_{\text{new}} = J^\star_{\text{after}}$$

**Theorem 4 (recovery upper bound).** Under column-localisation and separation $\mu > 0$, Hwang binary-splitting identifies $S$ and performs an exact rebuild in $\mathcal{O}(r \log(N/r))$ forward evaluations in expectation.

**Theorem 4 (recovery lower bound).** Any adaptive, randomised algorithm with forward block-test access (single-bit responses) must issue $\Omega(r \log(N/r))$ queries in expectation to identify $S$. The $\Omega(r)$ value-recovery floor applies even when $S$ is known.

The two bounds are tight. At $r = \mathcal{O}(1)$: cost $= \mathcal{O}(\log N)$, an exponential improvement over $\Theta(N)$ FD. At $r = \Theta(N)$: cost $= \Theta(N)$ — no cheaper method exists.

**BFS batching.** In practice, probes at each level of the binary-splitting tree are issued as a single batched `vmap` call. Sequential device round-trips therefore scale as the tree depth $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$, not the probe count $\mathcal{O}(r \log N)$ — the dominant cost on GPU.

A **density pre-screen** precedes the BFS: $\lceil \log_2 N \rceil + 3$ random unit-vector probes $\{e_i\}$ estimate the fraction of active columns. If this fraction exceeds $1/3$, the jump is too dense for group testing to be cheaper than FD, and the algorithm falls back immediately, spending only $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ probes before the fallback.

### 3b. Hybrid Routing

The Hybrid solver routes between the two archetypes using the density pre-screen as its diagnosis:

1. **Column-sparse path** (gear archetype): density screen below threshold → BFS group testing, exact rebuild in $\mathcal{O}(r \log(N/r))$ JVPs.
2. **Dense low-rank path** (contact archetype): density screen above threshold → for the dense rank-$\approx 1$ contact jump, the standard quasi-Newton secant update handles the remaining convergence. The screen spends $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ probes, then falls back — no full FD rebuild needed.
3. **Genuinely full-rank**: both paths reject via held-out verification → forward FD at cost $N+1$.

A held-out probe verifies each accepted model; a wrong route is rejected rather than trusted.

**Proposition (Hybrid cost bound).** The total recovery cost is bounded by:

$$C_{\text{hybrid}} \leq \min\!\bigl(\mathcal{O}(r \log(N/r)),\, \mathcal{O}(N)\bigr) + \mathcal{O}(\log N)$$

where the additive $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ term arises from the density pre-screen. For $N \leq 256$ this constant is at most 11 probes.

## 4. Online Regret

**Theorem 5 (regret synthesis).** Over a horizon of $T$ outer steps with $m$ rank-$r$ discontinuity events at unknown times, the adaptive pipeline incurs cumulative excess cost over a clairvoyant oracle of:

$$\mathrm{Reg}(T) = \mathcal{O}\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{mNT}{r}}\right) + \mathcal{O}\!\left(m \cdot r \log(N/r)\right) + \mathcal{O}(\alpha T \log N)$$

The three terms correspond to detection latency ($\Theta(N/k_p)$ steps per event), recovery cost ($\Theta(r \log(N/r))$ JVPs per event), and false-positive overhead ($\alpha$ false alarm rate, $\log N$ cost per alarm). The bound is order-optimal: increasing $k_p$ shifts mass from the first term to the third. For rank-$1$ events, detection dominates recovery; the quantitative case for the subspace detector over scalar MAD.

## 5. Evaluation

### Sparse Gear Events — Group-Testing Home Turf

Coupled chain task ($N = 16 \to 64$, gear fuzz $K = N/2$ columns, event multiplier $\times 10$, 5 seeds). At $N = 64$, the plain Adaptive solver achieves $158$ JVPs against FD's $1{,}628$ — a **10.3× reduction** — reflecting a single well-timed FD rebuild on a problem where the coupling does not trigger instability. Group Testing achieves $329$ JVPs ($5.0\times$) at all sparsity levels, invariant to jump magnitude over a $\times 2 \to \times 50$ range.

### Contact Events — Dense Rank-1 Jumps

Arm task, single tip-contact, $N = 4 \to 12$, 5 seeds. GroupTest prevails not by localising contact columns (the jump is column-dense) but because the $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ density screen correctly identifies the dense archetype, routes to a cheap secant continuation, and avoids a full FD rebuild entirely.

The randomised Rank-R (Nyström) rebuild — the theoretically motivated tool for low-rank dense jumps — costs $2.3\text{–}2.5\times$ FD at $N = 4 \to 12$: randomised reconstruction overhead dominates at small $N$ where per-recovery savings are modest. At $N \gtrsim 50$ this overhead is expected to amortise; it is untested at that scale.

### Hybrid Stability Across Dimension

The coupled chain (off-diagonal Jacobian energy $\approx 0.40$, condition number growing linearly with $N$) exposes the failure mode of plain Adaptive at $N = 32$: $1{,}046$ JVPs against FD's $827$. The coupling-induced instability arises from repeated rank-1 secant corrections fighting a structurally inconsistent Jacobian, not from any asymptotic complexity change. The Hybrid solver, routing through the density screen, resolves this at $198$ JVPs ($4.2\times$ below FD). At $N = 64$, Hybrid achieves $320$ JVPs ($5.1\times$) while Adaptive recovers to $158$ JVPs ($10.3\times$) — coupling ceases to trigger instability at this scale.

## 6. Scope and Limitations

**Noise.** All methods fail to converge at any observation noise $\sigma > 0$ (0/30 seeds). The root-finding iterate diverges because the residual is fundamentally noisy; adjusting the convergence tolerance reduces JVP count while failing, but does not restore convergence. The EWMA detector reduces cost-while-failing by 14–20% against MAD-based Adaptive. Noise-robust root-finding requires a CUSUM companion or projected-gradient outer loop and is not addressed here.

**Scaling beyond $N = 64$ on CPU.** GroupTest and Hybrid time out at $N \geq 128$ on sparse fuzz (BFS compilation overhead accumulates across $\mathcal{O}(\log N)$ tree levels, each requiring a separate `vmap` shape). At $N = 128$ with dense fuzz the density pre-screen triggers a fast FD fallback; this still converges at $638$ JVPs ($5.0\times$ vs FD's $3{,}229$). GPU deployment eliminates the compilation bottleneck; results at $N \geq 128$ are reserved for that context.

**Contact recovery at large $N$.** Rank-R is untested above $N = 12$. The theory predicts the overhead amortises; empirical validation is pending.
